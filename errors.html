L5 · Errors and calibration

From intensity noise to slope error; random versus systematic; which calibration removes which error.

Learning objective
Follow an error from the camera to the slope: intensity noise → phase → screen position → slope. Separate random errors (averaged down) from systematic ones (removed only by calibration), and match each calibration to the error it removes.

Check yourself
1. Which reduces random slope error more: doubling zd or doubling N?

Doubling zd halves σθ (σθ ∝ 1/zd); doubling N reduces it by only √2. But a longer zd needs a larger screen (D1).

2. Why does SMOTS reach sub-microradian noise with an ordinary monitor?

It averages over every pixel of a segment's aperture: the phase noise falls roughly as 1/√M, and Choi et al. also use a long zd (2020 mm).

3. A 5 mm error in a 2020 mm zd: is it random or systematic, and how big is its effect on a 1 mrad tilt?

Systematic: a scale error of 5/2020 = 0.25 %, i.e. 2.5 µrad on 1 mrad. Averaging cannot remove it; only a better distance measurement or calibration can.
