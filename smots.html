R1 · SMOTS — Choi et al. 2017

From the paper to the code: method, equations, benches and replication status.

R1The paper

H. Choi, I. Trumper, M. Dubin, W. Zhao and D. W. Kim, "Simultaneous angular alignment of segmented mirrors using sinusoidal pattern analysis," Proc. SPIE 10377, 103770G (2017), doi: 10.1117/12.2275197. It builds on the same group's Optics Express paper, vol. 25, 18152 (2017).

A monitor shows one sinusoidal pattern; a camera photographs its reflection in every segment of a mirror array at once. When a segment tilts, the pattern seen in it shifts. Subtracting a reference image and comparing the two in the Fourier domain gives that shift — one number per segment — and the tilt follows from the geometry. There is no phase unwrapping and no slope integration, which is what makes the method fast.

R1.3The paper's bench against the ASU bench
Table 3 – Experimental parameters. Paper values from Sec. 3 and 3.1; ASU values from the Week 11 camera report. Derived rows are computed live.

Reading the table. The two benches are an order of magnitude apart in screen distance, yet the angle represented by one fringe period, Pf/(2zd), is similar. The ASU bench compensates its short distance with a finer screen and a finer fringe.

The shorter distance has two costs. One screen pixel corresponds to a larger angle (lower sensitivity per pixel, L1), and the mirror and the screen image are much closer together, so they cannot both be in focus — the paper's hyperfocal condition (Sec. 2) is not reachable at 432 mm (D3, Step 5).
