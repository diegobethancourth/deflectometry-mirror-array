L1 · Reflection and surface slope

Why a screen and a camera measure the slope of a mirror.

Learning objective
After this module you can explain why deflectometry measures surface slope, predict how far the reflected screen point moves for a given tilt, and state the slope resolution set by one screen pixel.

Key ideas
  • The measured quantity is the slope (surface normal), not the height. Height comes later, by integration (L4).
  • A tilt θ turns the reflected ray by 2θ; at distance zd the screen point moves by about 2θ·zd. A longer zd means more screen motion per microradian — higher sensitivity.
  • One screen pixel of motion corresponds to a slope of p/(2zd); fringe phase (L2, L3) locates the screen point to a small fraction of a pixel.
Check yourself
1. Doubling zd changes the screen shift for a given tilt how?

It doubles it: Δx ≈ 2θ·zd. The slope resolution per pixel, p/(2zd), halves — the measurement becomes twice as sensitive.

2. Why can deflectometry not measure a pure piston (a mirror moved straight up)?

A piston does not change the surface normal, so every ray reflects in the same direction; the screen points barely move. Deflectometry is a slope measurement.

3. Set a curvature with zero tilt. Why do the screen points spread or converge?

The slope now varies across the mirror (slope = x/R), so each ray is turned by a different 2·slope; the reflected fan widens (convex) or narrows (concave).

4. With p = 89.6 µm and zd = 300 mm, what slope moves the screen point by one pixel?

p/(2zd) = 0.0896/600 = 149 µrad. Phase measurement to 1/100 of a fringe of 13 pixels brings this to about 19 µrad (see L5).
